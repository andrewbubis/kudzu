Shipping & returns

Last updated 19 August 2026

Every original on this site is packed and posted by the artist who made it. That is slower than a warehouse and better for the work. Here is exactly what to expect.

When it ships

The artist has ten business days from the moment you pay to get your piece into the post. You are told the exact date at checkout and again in your confirmation email, and the artist is given the same date.

Ten days is not slowness. An original arrives stretched, wrapped, corner-protected and crated by hand, often by someone doing it around a day job. Most pieces go sooner.

The day it goes out you get an email with a tracking number. If your date passes with no word, write to info@kudzuarts.com and we will chase the artist ourselves.

How it travels

  • Insured for the full purchase price.
  • Signature required on delivery. If nobody is home the carrier leaves a card rather than the work.
  • Tracked door to door.

Transit time depends on the carrier and the size of the piece. A small framed work may take a few days; a large crated canvas can take two or three weeks. Your tracking number is the accurate answer.

Shipping costs and customs

Shipping within the United States is included in the price shown. For international orders, any import duty, VAT or customs charge is the buyer's responsibility and is collected by the carrier on delivery — Kudzu Arts has no way to pay these in advance and no way to predict them.

Local pickup

Some artists offer collection in person. When they do, the option appears at checkout with the artist's city named. Choose it and you and the artist are introduced by email, with each other's details, and you arrange a time between you. When you meet you both sign a bill of lading on the artist's phone, and that signed document is emailed to you both as the receipt.

If it arrives damaged

Photograph the box before you open it any further, then photograph the work. Email both to info@kudzuarts.com within forty-eight hours of delivery.

Those photographs are what settle a claim. Without them a carrier will say the parcel left them in good condition, and there is very little anyone can do.

Where a work arrives damaged and is reported in time, you get a full refund including shipping, or a repair arranged with the artist if the piece can be properly restored and you would rather keep it. That is your choice, not ours.

If it is not what was described

Tell us within seven days of delivery. Photographs of the work as it arrived, alongside the listing, are what we need. Where a piece is materially different from its description — wrong dimensions, wrong medium, condition not disclosed — you get a full refund including return shipping, which we arrange.

Change of mind

Original artworks are one-of-a-kind and are not sold on approval, so we do not accept returns simply because a piece is not what you hoped once it is on your wall. This is normal for original art and it exists to protect artists, who cannot absorb a round trip on a unique work.

We would much rather you were sure first. Every artist on this site answers their own messages — ask for more photographs, a scale reference, a shot in daylight, or a video. Use the contact form on their page. Nobody will mind.

Cancelling before it ships

If you change your mind before the artist posts it, email info@kudzuarts.com straight away. If the work has not yet gone out we will cancel and refund you in full, and the piece goes back on the artist's page. Once it is in the post this is no longer possible.

Prints

Prints are made to order by our printing partner and are not returnable except where faulty or damaged, for the simple reason that nothing exists until you order it. Report any fault within fourteen days and we will reprint it.

How refunds are paid

Refunds go back to the card you paid with, usually within five to ten business days depending on your bank. We refund the full amount you were charged.

Talk to us first

If something has gone wrong, please write to info@kudzuarts.com before opening a dispute with your bank. A dispute freezes the artist's money for months and is decided by people who have never seen the work. We would rather just fix it.
