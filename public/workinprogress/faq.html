How it works

Kudzu Arts is where Southern artists sell their work directly to the people who want it. Artists set their own prices and keep 75%.

What is Kudzu Arts?

A place where Southern artists list the work they have available, and you buy it from them directly.

There is no dealer in the middle marking the work up, and nobody deciding on your behalf what is worth showing you. The artist sets the price, keeps the piece until it sells, and is paid the moment it does.

Is Kudzu a gallery?

No. A gallery takes work on consignment, sets the price, and sells it on the artist's behalf — typically for 40 to 50% of the sale.

40–50% What a traditional gallery commission takes from the artist
25% What Kudzu takes. The artist keeps the rest, and sets the price themselves

That 25% covers promotion, the cost of running the sale, and underwrites Kudzu's programming — workshops, residencies and public art. Nothing is consigned: the artist holds the physical work until a buyer has paid for it.

Artists apply to join. What appears on their page, and what it costs, is theirs.

How do I buy something?

Find a piece and buy it. Payment runs through Stripe and goes to the artist's own account — not into a Kudzu balance that somebody has to release.

You enter your address before you pay, so you see the real shipping cost for your address rather than an estimate or a flat rate. Nothing is charged until you've seen the full total.

How does shipping work?

Shipping is priced live against the packed size and weight of the actual piece and the address it's going to, and added on top. It is never taken out of the artist's share.

Every original ships insured for its full sale price and signature-required. Kudzu buys the postage, so the artist packs the work and hands it over — they never pay at the counter and never wait to be paid back.

Some artists also offer local pickup. You arrange a time between you, and you both sign a receipt at the handoff which is emailed to each of you.

Where do you ship?

The United States and Canada.

Somewhere else? Write to info@kudzuarts.com and we'll quote it by hand. A one-of-a-kind painting travelling a long way deserves a conversation rather than a checkbox.

What if something arrives damaged?

Tell us within 72 hours of delivery, with photographs. Every original is insured for what you paid, and we handle the claim rather than leaving it between you and the artist.

Otherwise sales are final — these are singular objects, not stock.

Still wondering something?

Ask us directly — a person reads it.

What does it cost me?

Nothing to join, nothing monthly, and nothing when a piece doesn't sell.

Kudzu takes 25% when a work sells. You keep 75% of the price you set — against the 40 to 50% a gallery would take for setting the price itself.

Shipping doesn't come out of your share either. The buyer pays it on top, and Kudzu buys the label.

When do I get paid?

At the moment of purchase. Not in thirty days, not after delivery, not once someone remembers to send it.

You connect your own Stripe account and the buyer's payment goes straight into it, with Kudzu's commission taken in transit. Kudzu never holds your money at any point — there is no balance to chase and nothing to invoice for. That's true of a posted sale and a local pickup alike.

Do you take my work?

No. Nothing is consigned and nothing is shipped to us. You keep every piece in your own studio until somebody buys it, and then it goes straight from you to them.

What do I have to do when something sells?

Pack it and hand it over.

We buy the postage and send you a code — you show it at the post office on your phone and they print the label at the counter. No printer, nothing to pay, nothing to claim back. The label is already insured for the sale price and marked signature-required.

For a local pickup you meet the buyer, both sign a receipt on your phone, and it's emailed to both of you as the record of delivery.

Can I take my work back down?

Whenever you like, for any reason. A piece is exclusive to Kudzu only while it is listed here, and that ends the moment you remove it.

The one thing we ask is good faith: if a piece comes down and then sells within six months to somebody who first found it through Kudzu, bring that sale back through us. It rests on mutual respect rather than surveillance.

How do I join?

Kudzu is invite-based. Once you have an invite you'll:

  • connect a Stripe account, so buyers pay you directly
  • add a photo, a bio and a C.V.
  • read and sign the artist agreement
  • upload work, set your own prices, and go live

Up to ten pieces live at once, so a page stays a considered selection rather than an inventory.

Want to be considered?

Send us your work — tell us what you make and where you make it.
